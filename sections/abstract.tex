Drones show great potential for expanding the reach of data collection into remote, 
hazardous, or otherwise difficult to reach areas. However, their use is limited by their high power 
consumption and resulting short mission time. One method of extending a drone's mission time is through
perching behavior, in which a drone is capable of attaching to a stationary object or surface while cutting 
off power to its motors. Various methods of implementing this behavior have been researched, though many 
are mechanically complex, require complex controls, or are only suitable for very small drones. 
Additionally, means to detect a perch are needed as assistance when an operators line-of-sight is limited,
or to enable full autonomy. Few detection methods were found in the background literature, and those that were 
often depended on expensive components. 
This thesis designed a perch grasping and detection mechanism that can be implemented with low-cost components.
The grasping device was verified to support a mass of at least 1.17kg, and function over at least 100 cycles.
The detection method was verified, under ideal conditions, to detect perch distance with less than 
75mm and 25 degrees of error in distance and orientation, respectively. Though additional work is required to
improve the reliability of perch detection against different background scenes, and improve the stability of 
perch orientation estimation, the proposed detection method showed promise for use in autonomous drone perching.
