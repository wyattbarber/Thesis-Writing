Drones show great potential for expanding the reach of data collection into remote, hazardous, or otherwise difficult to reach areas. 
For example understanding forest canopy ecology is critical to maintaining a healthy global ecosystem, 
though data collection in dense forest canopies is difficult. Current methods of collecting data from forest canopies are
expensive and labor-intensive. Drones have potential to aid in data collection from forest canopies, 
however the noise they produce makes them unsuitable for environmental monitoring. 
They are also limited in mission time
due to their high power consumption.
Drones need a method of perching in a low power mode in order to perform environmental data collection.
Some methods of doing this have been developed, however most are bulky and mechanically complex, 
or require complex maneuvering of the drone in order to attach it to a perch.
This thesis aimed develop an actuator that can attach a drone or small payload to a perch, remain attached passively, 
and detach itself under its own power.
