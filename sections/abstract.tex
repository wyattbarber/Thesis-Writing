Perching mechanisms in drones can allow extended mission times, noise reduction during data collection,
and aid in placing sensors in inaccessible locations. In addition to perching mechanisms, methods of detecting perches
are needed to support autonomy or aid operators in limited line-of-sight.
%
%
This thesis designed a perch grasping and detection mechanism for grasping overhead objects such as tree branches or
powerlines. The grasping device was designed to be simple to fabricate on a laser cutter with wood or plastic, and 
to remain passively attached after perching is complete. The detection mechanism was implemented with stereo vision, 
to make use of low-cost camera sensors and open-sourced image processing tools. 
%
%
The grasping device was verified under controlled, indoor conditions to support a drone with mass of at least 1.17kg, 
and function over at least 100 cycles.
The detection method was verified, under ideal conditions, to detect perch distance with less than 
75mm and 25 degrees of error in distance and orientation, respectively. 
%
%
The proposed detection method showed promise for use in autonomous drone perching, though further work is needed to 
refine the concept. In particular the method used is very sensitive to patterns and colors of the background, which can 
prevent detection of a perch. Additionally, the estimated orientation of a perch becomes more unreliable the closer a perch is,
posing issues for integrating the detection mechanism into a flight controller.
