\subsection{Introduction}

Much recent research has been dedicated to developing multi-modal drones that can transition between flight
and a stationary mode attached to a structure. Often referred to as "perching", this behavior is intended
to increase the flexibility of how drones can be used \cite{meng_aerial_2022}. Perching may be used to extend 
mission time when a drone does not need to be in flight for the entirety of its use, for example if used for
sensor deployment \cite{liu_perching_2022,kirchgeorg_hedgehog_2022}. In some cases, such as if used for power line
inspection, they may even recharge while perched \cite{hoang_autonomous_2024,kitchen_design_2020}. Perching may extend
a drone's mission time through inclement weather in which it would be unable to fly \cite{liu_perching_2022}. Drones 
equipped with perching mechanisms can be used to deploy sensors in areas that are inaccessible or hazardous for 
humans to reach \cite{liu_mini-drone_2023,seiber_tracking_2018}.

There are many designs for devices to enable perching, ranging from simple grippers that attach to an object and use
mechanical pressure to hold a drone in place \cite{kirchgeorg_soft_2023,chi_optimized_2014}, to biomimetic bird claw 
inspired designs \cite{roderick_bird-inspired_2021}. Some designs use micro scale spikes \cite{roderick_bioinspired_2017}, 
electrostatics \cite{graule_perching_2016}, or electromagnetics \cite{liu_perching_2022} to attach to specific surfaces.
One proposed mechanism even uses a grapple suspended beneath a drone by a tether, with the drone looping around the 
perch to attach \cite{nguyen_passively_2019}.

Each of the varied mechanisms has different benefits and drawbacks. Electrostatic and electromagnetic based devices
only work on specific materials, though they can be used to attach a drone to a vertical surface instead of only above 
or beneath a perch \cite{meng_aerial_2022,liu_perching_2022,graule_perching_2016}. Claw like designs can attach to a wider 
variety of surfaces, though typically only from a specific orientation, and they have limits on the size and shape of perch they can grasp. 
If designed correctly, a claw like perching mechanism can double as a manipulator to carry 
and release additional payloads \cite{meng_aerial_2022,roderick_bird-inspired_2021}.

The form of grasping mechanism can influence the complexity of control algorithms required to perform perching. 
Bird like mechanisms also require that the drone perform a specific approach to the perch, mimicking how a bird
would land \cite{roderick_bird-inspired_2021}. The grappling based mechanism requires the drone to perform an approach 
from above the perch, loop around to the bottom once the grapple is engaged, and then reverse the procedure to 
resume flight.

Also of importance is a method for detecting suitable perches. A mechanism designed to grasp a perch is sensitive to how
it approaches and lands on the perch \cite{roderick_bird-inspired_2021,kirchgeorg_hedgehog_2022}. A human operator may struggle
to correctly align a drone to a perch without a semi-autonomous guidance function, as the distance between them and the drone may be 
large and not provide a good viewing angle for gauging drone orientation and distance relative to the perch, even if the operator has line-of-sight. 
Most proposed methods use stereo cameras to detect specific geometries such as thin boards or cylinders. These methods depend on relatively 
expensive stereo cameras to measure quality depth data \cite{kominami_detection_2023,von_frankenberg_detection_2020}.

Despite the research interest in creating perching drones, little evidence of these designed being put to practical use could be found. 
It may be that the proposed designs are complex and inaccessible to researchers who would put them to use in ecology, infrastructure inspection,
disaster response, or any of the other fields where such devices have been suggested to provide value. 
Design of perching mechanisms that are simple to fabricate, allow for simple flight controllers to execute the approach to a perch, 
and provide detection with low-cost components and open-sourced software can expand the accessibility of using perching drones in research.


\subsection{Purpose}

This thesis aims to design a novel perch detection method that can detect cylindrical perches such as branches or power lines, 
as well as a grasping device that can attach a drone underneath such an object. The device should use low-cost components and accessible
software libraries to keep the proposed design affordable and accessible. This thesis will verify the reliability and accuracy
of the proposed detection method and the strength, and weight of the grasping mechanism. 


\subsection{Scope}

The scope of this thesis is limited to designing and verifying a perch detection and grasping device under controlled 
lab conditions. The device will not be integrated with a drone and no drone will be used in testing.


\subsection{Assumptions}

This thesis assumes that the device may need to support a drone with a mass of up to 500g, plus 31.58 N
of dynamic load, for a total static load of 3.72 kg.

The dynamic load is to allow for force introduced by motion of the perch, such as a swaying tree branch.
Assuming that the drone is perched in a tree and subject to sinusoidal motion, its maximum acceleration is estimated at $63.17 m/s^2$ 
based on data provided by Kamimura et al. (0.4 m max displacement with a frequency of 2 Hz) \cite{kamimura_energy_2024}.  
The referenced study did use only Japanese Cedar, which may not
be representative of all trees. However, similar data for other species could not be found.


\subsection{Significance}

Perching can increase the versatility of drones in data collection and inspection tasks, allowing them to achieve longer mission times
and deploy sensors in otherwise inaccessible locations. Novel and affordable detection ad grasping methods such as those proposed in this thesis can
make perching more accessible for a wider variety of applications.
