\subsubsection{Latch Mechanism}

The chosen design for the grasper consists of a rotating clasp, driven by a continuous rotation, speed-controlled hobby servo motor. 
A rendering of the design is shown in Fig. \ref{fig:clasp-design}, and the OpenSCAD source used to create it can be found in appendix \ref{app:grasper-scad}.
The \texttt{BOSL2} library \cite{belfryscad_bosl2_nodate} was used to create the gear components.

The device in Fig. \ref{fig:clasp-design} here has an inner radius of 35mm, which results in it being able to accept a perch of up to 
50mm diameter. The outer radius of the device is 50mm. The latch component (green object in Fig. \ref{fig:clasp-design})
has a radial width of 7.4mm. The code in appendix \ref{app:grasper-scad} is parameterized such that the grasper can easily
be modified to grow to accommodate larger perches, or be shrunk to work with smaller perches while saving weight and space.

It is worth noting that this device will not prevent the device from swinging, that is it provides no means 
to prevent the drone from rotating about the y-axis once the drone is perched. This thesis assumes that the 
application of this device does not require the drone to remain totally stationary, only that it be supported 
so that it can power off its motors without falling. This is different from some other grasping designs, which 
apply pressure to the perch to prevent rotation.

\subsubsection{Controller} \label{sec:methods:controller}

A Raspberry Pi Compute Module 4 was used to control the servos on the grasper device. To avoid 
complicating the mechanical design and adding weight with limit switches to detect open and closed states, 
an Adafruit ADS1015 analog-digital converter was used to measure the current drawn by each servo.
The current measurement is used to detect when the servo has stalled, indicating 
that the limits of travel are reached and the latch has either fully opened or closed.

In addition to current monitoring, time limits are used during opening and closing the grasper to avoid stopping early 
due to erroneous current measurements, or not stopping after the limit is released due to current measurement
being erroneously low. Failure to stop after the limit was reached in particular seemed to accelerate drift of 
the servos zero point. This drift would lead to the grasper becoming unresponsive to commands, or moving in the
wrong direction, after roughly 20 cycles. Time limits such that the grasper would not stop opening or closing
until at least 200ms had elapsed, and would automatically stop if 2s had elapsed, were implemented to reduce 
the rate of zero point drift.

The schematic of the servo control module can be seen in appendix \ref{app:schematics},
Fig. \ref{fig:clasp-ctl}. The schematic showing the compute module and its interface to the
servo controller can be seen in appendix \ref{app:schematics},
Fig. \ref{fig:cpu-module}.


\subsection{Grasper Verification}

The achieved holding capacity of the device (goal 1 of Table \ref{tab:design-goals})
was verified with the following test process:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The grasper was closed around a test perch, suspended above the ground.
    \item A bag was attached to the underside of the grasper, close to the center of its baseplate in the x-y plane.
    \item Weights were added to the bag incrementally, with a 5-minute period between each addition.
    \item Once the device breaks before the 5-minute period has expired after the most recent addition, or the maximum available weight of
        8.83kg is reached, the holding capacity is determined to be the load weight at the previous successful weight addition.
\end{enumerate}

To avoid damaging the electronic components when the device failed, all except the servos were removed for the duration of this test. 
The device is capable of remaining attached without power to the servos, and the weight of the removed electronics was removed from the
final weight at the end of the test. The test setup is shown in Fig. \ref{fig:capacity-test}, 
and the results are summarized in Section \ref{sec:results:capacity}.

    \includegraphics[width=0.66\textwidth]{sections/methods/capacity-test.jpg}
    \caption{The test setup for determining the holding capacity of the grasper, showing the grasper with 
    electronics removed and a shopping bag, where the test weights were placed, suspended from the base of the grasper.}
    \label{fig:capacity-test}
\end{figure}

Goal 2 of Table \ref{tab:design-goals} was verified by weighing the individual components of the completed device.
The measured weights are recorded in table \ref{tab:weights}.

To verify that the proposed design can open and close reliably, the opening and closing process was manually 
repeated 100 times. Each time after closing the grasper or opening it again, the device was 
visually inspected to ensure that the latch did reach its fully closed and opened positions.
If opening or closing was not successful, then that test was marked as a failure, and the specific reason recorded.
The success rate and failure causes are shown in Section \ref{sec:results:repeatability}.
