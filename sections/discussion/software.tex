\subsection{Perch Detection Success Rate}

The results show that the proposed image processing method has potential, though performance is dependent on the background.
Only about 2/3 of cases are correctly detected with the zigzag background, compared to every case against a white background.
Interestingly the white background performed better than the pattern, despite the white background typically being more
difficult for the \texttt{StereoSGBM} algorithm to process. It seems that, in the white background case, 
the background removal is effective enough to compensate for poor performance of the block matching. 
This is illustrated in Fig. {fig:fitler-compare}, which shows point clouds generated with and without
background removal enabled.

The lower performance of the patterned background appears to be the result of two primary causes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The thresholding based background filter is less effective when the background contains dark regions.
    \item The Hough vote threshold is low, causing small background noise artifacts to be detected as valid lines.
\end{enumerate}

Issue 1 requires better foreground isolation to resolve, or better quality discrepancy maps. Improving the stereo discrepancy
significantly may prove difficult while using off-the-shelf, free and open sourced software, especially while attempting to keep processing 
speed high and suitable for real-time controls. Foreground isolation could perhaps be improved by incorporating time series data, such as optical
flow, to isolate foreground by checking how object move relative to the drone. This would depend on the drone being in motion. Machine learning
methods could also prove useful. 

Issue 2 seems simple to fix by raising the Hough vote threshold, however retesting the data with a higher value showed that there is a trade-off
between performance in the flat white background vs the zigzag background; raising the minimum vote parameter causes the target object to not be 
detected in the simple white background case because it doesn't have enough points remaining to reach the threshold. Further experimentation may
be needed to determine optimal parameters for different background types. 

Fig. {fig:issues-background} illustrates these issues. The correct object is detected, indicating that the processing pipeline
is not completely failing, however another region of points is also getting detected as a line and is selected as the best
candidate because it is closer. Fig. {fig:issues-background:disp} shows that this object is an erroneous high disparity 
region. This suggests that the issues present here may simply be a result of suboptimal calibration. Table \ref{tab:success-rates-dist}
shows that all failures were isolated to the 2000mm test case, which supports that the issue is due to how that specific scene
layout interacts with the stereo calibration and matching.
